# AMPX - Amprius research assessment

Audience: investor researching a hypothetical $10,000 exposure. Date: September 5, 2026. Scope: 3-5 years.

### Why the business is interesting

Batteries can be a decisive constraint in aircraft and other weight-sensitive products. If a cell provides useful extra endurance at an acceptable cost, customers can justify a premium. A focused supplier can build a worthwhile business without winning the entire electric-vehicle market. The key economic question is how much of that premium Amprius retains after manufacturing, support, warranty and development costs.

### Why the stock requires caution

A high starting sales multiple means a large part of future success is already anticipated. Revenue can grow quickly while per-share returns disappoint if margins stall, competitors reduce pricing, or the share count rises. The scenario model below makes those effects explicit.

**10× outlook:** not supported by the current 3–5 year evidence. The modeled bull case reaches about 4.2× and already requires substantial commercial scale, strong differentiation and limited dilution.

### Separate an expensive exit from recurring needs

The 10-Q attributes $20 million of operating cash outflow to terminating the Colorado lease. Removing it, then including capital spending, gives an illustrative first-half cash use of $22.93 million. At an unchanged rate, June cash would cover about 19.5 months. This is a sensitivity calculation, not a forecast or a guarantee of funding.

Working capital can absorb money even when the income statement improves. Growing receivables may mean payment terms or shipment timing, rather than bad debts; collections must verify that explanation. Inventory can support future deliveries, but also creates exposure to delays and obsolescence. Neither balance alone establishes misconduct.

### The second half has work to do

Using first-half revenue of $62.568 million, meeting the revenue floor requires $77.432 million in H2, averaging $38.716 million each quarter. Achieving the full-year gross-margin floor at that sales level requires roughly 31.2% H2 gross margin. These are derived requirements, not management quarterly guidance.

The company also targets at least $4 million adjusted EBITDA for 2026. Adjusted EBITDA excludes costs such as stock compensation and is not free cash flow. A durable improvement would show customer collections and cash generation alongside accounting profitability.

Interpretation: immediate insolvency is not the central case supported by this snapshot. Self-funded multi-year growth is nevertheless unproven. Financing from options or warrants depends on circumstances and should not be assumed to recur.

## Technology and the competitive moat

*Performance matters only when it survives production and customer use*

### The distinction between product platforms matters

The annual filing describes SiMaxx production in Fremont and SiCore based on Berzelius materials and partner manufacturing. More than 2 GWh of accessible partner capacity is not owned capacity or measured output. Berzelius material exclusivity covers the U.S., Canada and Mexico; marketing is nonexclusive, and the supplier may compete. Procurement terms are agreed through purchase orders. [Source: 2025 Form 10-K.]

My assessment: qualification expertise, cell design, customer service and manufacturing coordination may create value, but a patent count or performance headline does not demonstrate pricing power. The supplier relationship weakens a simple story in which Amprius exclusively owns the entire commercial advantage.

### SiCore500 is a milestone to verify

The September announcement specifies 500 Wh/kg at 1C continuous discharge, with availability expected in Q4 2026. Initial production is planned in Fremont, followed by partners. It explicitly says earlier cells above 500 Wh/kg never reached commercial volume. [Source: SiCore500 announcement.]

This is a credible research direction, but not proof of high-volume manufacturing. The announcement does not establish the new cell's commercial yield, delivered cost, cycle-life distribution or completed customer qualifications. Cell-level energy density also differs from a complete battery pack, which adds enclosure, interconnects and control systems.

Do not combine maximum energy density, fastest charging, longest cycle life and maximum discharge power from different cell designs. Useful comparisons require the same test conditions, operating temperature, discharge rate, usable capacity and end-of-life criterion.

### Domestic supply is a pathway, not yet a complete answer

The Nanotech Energy partnership identifies a 21700 cylindrical cell with 6.8 Ah capacity and 320 Wh/kg, and names L3Harris as a customer supporting the rationale. The release does not disclose Amprius-specific domestic shipment volume, yield or cost. It strengthens the production pathway without establishing that every component is independent of overseas sourcing. [Source: Nanotech announcement.]

The annual filing warns that qualifying components for defense sourcing requirements may fail or cost more than expected. The commercial test is acceptable performance and profit after complying with customer requirements, not simply the existence of a U.S. factory.

## Customers, competition and dilution

*The quality of revenue and the ownership denominator both matter*

### Customer evidence and its limits

Amprius reports a $24 million European drone order and a Stark Future opportunity exceeding $100 million over multiple years, beginning in 2027. These are not interchangeable with recognized sales or guaranteed backlog. [Source: Q2 announcement.]

Nordic Wing selected SiCore for its ASTERO drone after qualification, with Amprius reporting longer flight endurance. A named application supports product utility; it does not establish independent test replication, profitability or repeat-order economics. [Source: Nordic Wing announcement.]

The 10-Q reports $36.5 million remaining performance obligations, including grants and excluding uncommitted contracts. Three customers contributed 39% of H1 revenue; two represented 54% of June receivables. The annual filing reports $33 million of 2025 shipments to Ukraine-based customers, about 45% of sales. Customer and conflict-related demand concentration therefore deserve attention.

### A rival does not need to match every specification

CATL claims 350 Wh/kg mass-produced automotive cells and flight validation of 500 Wh/kg aviation systems. These are manufacturer claims, not independent comparisons with Amprius. They do show why exclusive technological leadership should not be assumed. [Source: CATL technology announcement.]

An alternative that is good enough, cheaper or easier to qualify can reduce Amprius's pricing power. Conversely, sourcing eligibility and specialized customer support can matter more than a competitor's best laboratory number. A useful moat would appear as repeat business and retained margins despite improving alternatives.

### Dilution: fewer warrants does not mean no dilution

June shares were 146.081 million versus 134.537 million at year-end, up 8.6%. Outstanding potential securities totaled 21.888 million: warrants, options and RSUs. Public/private warrants have an $11.50 exercise price; PIPE warrants $12.50, with September 2027 expiry. These potential shares are not all current dilution. [Source: 10-Q.]

May's exchange converted 7.128 million warrants into 2.727 million shares: less issuance than full exercise, but no exercise cash for that exchange. [Source: warrant announcement.]

Modeling principle: do not add the remaining warrants to current shares while also ignoring exercise proceeds, or count exchanged warrants twice. Future share counts in the scenario section are scenario assumptions, not a reported fully diluted capitalization.

The multiples are judgment calls. At 10% operating margin, 4x sales equals 40x operating profit before tax; even the middle assumption is demanding. A 6x sales valuation at 15% operating margin also equals 40x operating profit. If margins or growth fade, lower multiples are plausible.

### A growing company can still produce a losing stock

At $500 million revenue, 3x sales, zero net cash and 180 million shares, the model gives $8.33 per share: about 16% below the reference price despite substantial business growth. That is the practical risk of paying for success in advance.

A 10× share-price outcome would imply $98.90 per share. With 180 million future shares, that requires about $17.8 billion of equity value; at 6× sales and zero net debt, approximately $3.0 billion of annual revenue. That is well above the report's $1.2 billion bull case, so 10× is not a supported planning assumption within this horizon.

## What would change the assessment

*A research checklist for the next several reporting periods*

Evidence to watch | Stronger case | Weaker case
--- | --- | ---
Customer conversion | Repeat orders and paid deliveries | Large announcements without collection
Economics | Gross profit funds overhead and growth | Sales rise while losses persist
Cash conversion | Receivables turn into cash | Cash use persists after one-time costs
New products | SiCore500 qualified at repeatable yields | Launch delays or unacceptable trade-offs
Supply chain | Qualified supply with disclosed output | Cost increases, supplier dependence
Per-share progress | Sales and cash generation outgrow shares | Financing repeatedly offsets growth

### The next useful evidence

The next quarterly filing should clarify collections, ongoing cash needs and progress toward the annual outlook. The expected Q4 product availability and planned 2027 customer expansion are milestones, not guarantees. Reassess them when reported results arrive; a long holding period does not make a broken thesis recover automatically.

### Concentration changes the consequences

For illustration, $10,000 is 20% of a $50,000 portfolio and 2% of a $500,000 portfolio. A 75% loss would reduce those portfolios by 15% and 1.5%, respectively, assuming other holdings were unchanged. These are risk illustrations, not suggested position sizes. Funds needed for a fixed near-term expense have a different risk capacity from money with a flexible horizon.

### Confidence and unresolved questions

**Higher confidence:** reported financial history and capitalization arithmetic. **Lower confidence:** durable pricing power, future cash conversion, new-product production economics and terminal valuation. The middle scenario is not a most-likely forecast; reliable probabilities cannot be assigned from these disclosures.

The scope used SEC filings, issuer disclosures, a rival's technical announcement and dated market data. Product claims mostly come from interested manufacturers. No factory inspection, independent comparative testing, customer contract audit or patent-validity analysis was performed. The linked Q2 transcript and presentation were inaccessible through the research tool; neither was relied on.

Quote providers differed slightly ($9.89 versus $9.91); the dated Google Finance close anchors every calculation. The earnings release also shows a full-year weighted share assumption below already reported first-half shares; this potentially stale assumption was not used. June balances are not September balances. Taxes and currency conversion are outside this review.

**Overall assessment:** attractive product potential, meaningful commercial evidence, and a wide range of possible shareholder outcomes. Stronger proof of cash generation and defensible margins is needed before the evidence supports a high-confidence multi-year valuation case.
